# Backtest of the 2025 forecast against 13 years of NASS history

The 2025 forecast in `forecast/estimate_2025.py` was backtested on two years, 2023 and 2024. This page uses one
USDA NASS Quick Stats export to test its two load-bearing pieces over a longer history:

- **Grain.** The grain number is NASS's own latest pre-January forecast, and the grain interval is
  grain × (1 ± 2.06 × 0.0448), about ±9.2%. Here those are checked against 2012–2024.
- **Silage.** Naive silage baselines are scored over 2000–2024 to give context for method D.

Nothing here uses a 2025 value: the export has no 2025 rows. Data provenance is in [README.md](README.md), and every
table is in [outputs/tables.md](outputs/tables.md), generated by `backtest.py`.

## Grain: how much NASS's November number moves before the final (states, 2012–2024)

| set | state-years | median abs revision | RMS | 95th pct abs | inside the forecast's ±9.2% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nov forecast, 2012–2024 | 415 | 1.50% | **6.20%** | 13.4% | **91.3%** |
| Nov forecast, 2023–2024 only (the forecast's years) | 63 | 1.05% | 5.32% | 15.4% | 90.5% |
| states with no Nov forecast (latest of Oct/Sep/Aug/Jun) | 109 | 10.45% | 18.5% | 40.7% | 45.0% |
| Nov, state forecast under 500k acres | 142 | 2.56% | 9.23% | 20.0% | 82.4% |
| Nov, state forecast 500k–2M acres | 120 | 1.80% | 4.92% | 9.6% | 91.7% |
| Nov, state forecast 2M acres and over | 153 | 1.03% | 2.44% | 5.2% | 99.3% |

The forecast's `grain_revision_rel_sd` is 0.0448, an RMS about zero; the centred SD here is 6.15%.
`crosscheck_forecast.py` reproduces 0.0448 exactly from the forecast's own reports, on the same 63 state-years. On
those same state-years the Quick Stats finals give 5.32%. The gap comes from the finals: Quick Stats holds the
currently published value, and 13 of 31 states' 2024 grain finals were revised after the January 2025 print the
forecast used. The 2025 truth being graded is a *first* January print, so the revisions measured here, against
possibly later-revised finals, likely overstate the relevant error somewhat.

Coverage of the ±9.2% interval varies by year, from 96.9% (2012–2014) down to 84.4% in 2021 and 78.1% in 2022.

**Convergence through the season.** The balanced panel has 383 state-years in 33 states with all five forecasts.

| forecast | Jun Acreage | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| median abs revision | 4.49% | 3.64% | 2.78% | 1.49% | 1.49% |
| RMS | 9.36% | 8.25% | 7.47% | 6.38% | 6.38% |

The October and November state grain areas are identical in all 383 state-years that have both. The last in-season
change comes in October.

The 2000–2024 rows are context, not a head-to-head with method D: the years differ, and the finals source differs

persistence has the lower median and mean per-state error. That comparison covers only 2 years.

## What this means for the 2025 forecast

- **The grain interval is about right for large states and too narrow for small ones.** Over 13 years, 91% of
  November state forecasts land within ±9.2% of the final, short of the 95% target. To reach 95% with the same
  relative SD would take z ≈ 3.0, not 2.06. Re-estimating the SD on 2012–2024 (6.2%) gives ±12.8% and 94.5% coverage.
- **One relative SD does not fit all states.** Revisions shrink with state size: the RMS is 9.2% under 500k acres
  and 2.4% at 2M acres and over. The current interval covers 99% of the big states and 82% of the small ones. The US
  total is dominated by big states and moved at most 2.65% from November to final in any year 2012–2024.
- **The 2023–2024 window was a fairly typical, slightly calm sample.** The forecast's 0.0448 reproduces exactly
  from its own reports; the longer history is about 6%. 2021 and 2022 were the worst years (coverage 84% and 78%).
- **The forecast's grain backtest is flattered.** In 19 of its 82 grain backtest rows the state has no November
  number, so the code fills in the final itself and the error is zero by construction. These are the states that
  fall back to an earlier report (Sep in 2025), and in the export they revise a lot: median 10.5%, only 45% inside
  ±9.2%. Their 2025 grain values and intervals deserve the least trust.
- **Maryland's grain forecast is typical, and its interval looks adequate.** It is a ~400k-acre state with a
  4.6% Nov→final RMS, and 13 of 13 years fell inside ±9.2%. But revisions of 6–8% in either direction happened in
  4 of the last 10 years, so a final anywhere in about 375k–435k around the 405,000 forecast would not be unusual.
- **Method D does not clearly beat naive silage baselines.** On its own 2023–2024 rows it beats persistence on
  acre-weighted error but loses on median and mean. Over 25 years, persistence and the 3-year mean both run about
  10–11% acre-weighted, the same as D's 10.5%. That is honest context for presenting D: roughly as accurate as
  "same as last year". Its value is that it uses current planted and grain acres and carries an interval.
- **Maryland silage is hard to forecast precisely by any method.** The naive baselines miss by 11–15% (median 11%),
  partly from 5,000-acre rounding. The forecast's 27% CV and 10k–65k interval are wide, but not unreasonably so.
- **The convergence curve supports using the latest report.** Median error halves from June (4.5%) to October
  (1.5%), and November adds no new state area information. A September-only 2025 grain value carries about
  twice the median error of a November one.
